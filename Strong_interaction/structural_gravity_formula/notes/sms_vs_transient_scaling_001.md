# SMS vs transient-source scaling audit 001

## Scope

Compare a realistic rapidly accreting supergiant-protostar benchmark at

\[
M=10^4 M_\odot,\qquad R=100\,\mathrm{AU}
\]

with the transient excess-energy source descriptors already recorded for Xe KLL DR, N2 shape resonance, and CO2 shape resonance.

The 100 AU scale is representative of the bloated supergiant-protostar branch at M >= 10^4 Msun. It is not a generic radius for every 10^4 Msun stellar configuration.

## Scale descriptors

For any declared energy E and characteristic radius R, define diagnostic scales

\[
D_0=E,
\]

\[
D_1=\frac{E}{R},
\]

\[
D_2=\frac{E}{R^2},
\]

\[
D_3=\frac{E}{R^3}.
\]

These are dimensional diagnostics only. They are not DSD gravity laws.

For a spherical weak-field standard baseline,

\[
|\Phi|\sim \frac{G}{c^2}D_1,
\]

\[
g\sim \frac{G}{c^2}D_2,
\]

and for a uniform mean-density proxy,

\[
\bar\rho=\frac{3E}{4\pi c^2R^3},
\]

\[
\nabla^2\Phi=4\pi G\bar\rho=\frac{3G}{c^2}D_3.
\]

Therefore D1, D2, and D3 map respectively to potential-depth, surface-slope/acceleration, and local Poisson-source/curvature scales in the standard weak-field comparison.

## Main numerical result

For the 10^4 Msun, 100 AU SMS benchmark:

\[
\frac{E}{R}=1.19\times10^{38}\,\mathrm{J\,m^{-1}},
\]

\[
\frac{E}{R^2}=7.99\times10^{24}\,\mathrm{J\,m^{-2}},
\]

\[
\frac{E}{R^3}=5.34\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}.
\]

The mean mass-equivalent density is

\[
\bar\rho=1.42\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}.
\]

For the Xe53+ KLL DR transient excess descriptor using E=21.5 keV and the model radius R~=4.946 pm:

\[
\frac{E}{R}=6.96\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{J\,m^{-1}},
\]

\[
\frac{E}{R^2}=1.41\times10^8\,\mathrm{J\,m^{-2}},
\]

\[
\frac{E}{R^3}=2.85\times10^{19}\,\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}.
\]

Thus Xe/SMS ratios are approximately

\[
D_1:5.83\times10^{-42},
\]

\[
D_2:1.76\times10^{-17},
\]

\[
D_3:5.33\times10^7.
\]

This is the central scale-separation result: the transient Xe excess source is vastly smaller in potential-depth and acceleration scale, while vastly larger in local energy-density / Poisson-source scale.

For the N2 and CO2 diagnostic spherical proxies, D3 is respectively about 0.410 and 0.438 times the SMS mean D3, despite D1 and D2 being many orders of magnitude smaller.

## Interpretation

This comparison strongly warns against identifying source density with gravitational acceleration.

A source can have

- extremely high local D3,
- extremely small D2,
- and even smaller D1,

if its energy is confined to a sufficiently tiny region.

The structurally clean separation is therefore

\[
\text{total energy and size}\to\text{potential-depth scale},
\]

\[
\text{energy and size}\to\text{slope/acceleration scale},
\]

\[
\text{local energy distribution}\to\text{curvature/source-density scale}.
\]

This does not yet prove that DSD geometric distortion X obeys the weak-field mapping. The mapping is used only as the standard null baseline that a future DSD constitutive bridge must reproduce or explicitly depart from.

## Audit boundary

Do not compare the total rest mass of Xe53+ by dividing it by the ~5 pm resonance radius. The ion has a multiscale mass distribution: nuclear rest mass is concentrated on a femtometer scale while the captured electronic resonance occupies a picometer scale. The present Xe comparison is only for the transient excess-energy layer.

Likewise N2 and CO2 bond lengths are structural lengths, not measured 3D energy-density radii. Their D3 values remain diagnostic proxies only.
