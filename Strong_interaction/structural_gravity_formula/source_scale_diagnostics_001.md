# Source Scale Diagnostics 001

## Purpose

Keep dimensional source scales separate before proposing a DSD-specific gravitational constitutive bridge.

For an energy E associated with a declared characteristic scale R, define

\[
D_0=E,
\qquad
D_1=\frac{E}{R},
\qquad
D_2=\frac{E}{R^2},
\qquad
D_3=\frac{E}{R^3}.
\]

These are diagnostics, not DSD gravity laws.

## Standard weak-field reference mapping

For a spherical source in the ordinary weak-field baseline,

\[
|\Phi|\sim\frac{G}{c^2}D_1,
\]

\[
g\sim\frac{G}{c^2}D_2,
\]

while a mean-density Poisson source has

\[
\bar\rho=\frac{3}{4\pi c^2}D_3,
\]

\[
\nabla^2\Phi=4\pi G\bar\rho=\frac{3G}{c^2}D_3.
\]

Thus the standard baseline associates D1 with potential depth, D2 with slope/acceleration scale, and D3 with local source-density/curvature scale.

## DSD audit use

A future DSD geometric-distortion map X must not identify these scales without justification.

The minimum audit separation is

\[
X\quad\text{(distortion state/depth)},
\]

\[
\nabla X\quad\text{(spatial slope candidate)},
\]

\[
\nabla\nabla X\ \text{or}\ \nabla^2X\quad\text{(local curvature/source-response candidate)}.
\]

The 10^4 Msun SMS versus atomic/molecular transient comparison demonstrates that D3 can increase while D1 and D2 decrease by many orders of magnitude. Therefore local concentration cannot be identified directly with gravitational acceleration.

## Provenance rule

For multiscale systems, E and R must describe the same source layer. Do not divide a nuclear rest-energy baseline by an electronic orbital radius. Total-source and transient-excess diagnostics must remain separate.
